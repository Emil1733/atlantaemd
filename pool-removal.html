Deep Removal vs. GA Legal Abandonment

Full Demolition (Deep Removal)

1. Geotechnical Compaction & Soil Physics in Georgia Clay

The single greatest risk in any pool backfilling project is post-removal ground settling, or soil subsidence. The Piedmont plateau of North Georgia is dominated by Cecil clay loam (Georgia Red Clay). This clay soil is highly cohesive and undergoes severe volumetric changes depending on its moisture content. When a pool basin is backfilled with loose, uncompacted dirt, the clay particles act like a dry sponge. During Atlanta's heavy spring rains, the clay absorbs water, expanding in volume. During dry summer months, it shrinks and cracks, leaving air pockets that eventually collapse under gravity. This cyclic swelling and shrinking creates uneven lawn surfaces, deep sinkholes, and structural instability that ruins landscaping and prevents building.

To eliminate this risk, we follow the Standard Proctor Compaction Test (ASTM D698). We test the backfill soil to identify its Optimum Moisture Content (OMC)—the precise moisture level that allows the clay particles to pack together at their maximum dry density. During backfilling, we lay clean, organic-free structural fill in thin, horizontal layers (lifts) no deeper than 6 to 8 inches. We mechanically compact each lift using sheepsfoot and walk-behind vibratory rollers, applying high-frequency impact force to knead the clay and force out air pockets. We target a minimum of 95% Modified Proctor density, matching the load-bearing capacity of the surrounding virgin ground and keeping the yard stable for decades.

2. Subterranean Hydrology: Preventing the "Bathtub Effect"

A common error made by general grading contractors is failing to address the concrete pool floor, leading to the "Bathtub Effect." In partial pool removals (or abandonment), the top 2 to 3 feet of the pool walls are demolished and placed in the bottom of the shell. If the remaining pool bottom is left intact, the concrete basin acts as a giant, impermeable underground tub. Rainwater seeps down through the new backfill dirt but cannot penetrate the concrete shell. It pools in the basin, turning the soil into saturated, unstable mud.

In clay soils, this trapped water destroys the structural integrity of the backfill, creating a spongy lawn that stays wet year-round. Hydrostatic pressure from this trapped water can also migrate laterally, damaging neighboring retaining walls and home foundations. Our protocol prevents this by breaking the concrete barrier: we use heavy hydraulic hammers to punch 18-inch diameter drainage ports through the concrete pool floor every 4 feet across the basin, so groundwater can drain naturally into the water table. We also place a 12-to-18-inch base layer of washed #57 stone (crushed granite) at the bottom of the shell, which acts as a subterranean drainage channel, guiding water away from the compacted soil.

3. Environmental Staging & Arborist Compliance

Metro Atlanta is famous for its beautiful, mature tree canopy, and municipalities like Sandy Springs, Decatur, and Atlanta proper enforce strict tree preservation codes. Bringing heavy demolition equipment (like excavators and loaders) into residential backyards can cause soil compaction within the Critical Root Zone (CRZ) of mature oaks, pines, and maples. Soil compaction compresses the pore spaces in the soil, starving tree roots of oxygen and water, which leads to tree decline and death years later. In these municipalities, damaging a specimen tree without a permit can result in heavy municipal fines and stop-work orders.

We work closely with certified arborists to assess the root zones of specimen trees before bringing machinery onto the site. To prevent root damage, we build temporary access paths using 1.5-inch thick polymer protection mats that distribute weight and reduce ground pressure to less than 4 PSI. For narrow yards, we deploy mini-loaders with a 36-inch width profile that pass through standard gates without fence removal. Near mature trees, we use pneumatic air-spades to clear soil from around roots without cutting them, protecting your property's value and natural assets.

4. Permitting & County Regulatory Master Guide

Navigating the municipal permit process is essential to protect your home's title and value. Real estate transactions in Fulton, Cobb, and DeKalb Counties require disclosing any buried structures, such as abandoned pool shells. Performing a pool removal without a building permit is a code violation that can result in heavy fines, stop-work orders, and holds on property sales. We handle the entire permitting process on your behalf, coordinating with county and city inspectors.

The permitting process typically requires a detailed site plan showing property boundaries, easements, tree protection barriers, and erosion control measures (like Type C silt fencing). DeKalb, Fulton, and Cobb Counties require at least two inspections: a "Rough" inspection to verify the concrete pool bottom is punctured for drainage and the bond beam is removed, and a "Final" inspection after grading is complete. For engineered removals, we provide compaction logs and density certifications signed by a registered engineer, giving your builder the documentation needed to secure construction permits over the filled site.

5. How Long Pool Removal Takes in Atlanta

Most residential pool removals take three to five working days on site once the permit is issued. Partial removals are faster, often finishing in about 72 hours on site. Permit review is the variable most homeowners underestimate: depending on the county and the completeness of the site plan, approval can add one to three weeks before any equipment arrives.

Backyard access is the other major factor. A pool with a wide side yard and a straight path for a dump truck can be demolished and backfilled in three days. A pool behind a fence, a retaining wall or a mature tree canopy takes longer, because every load has to move through a narrower corridor and the tree protection staging adds setup time. Access and haul route should be confirmed during the site visit so the written estimate reflects the actual property.

7. When Removal Makes More Sense Than Repair

Not every old pool is worth removing, and not every repair is worth doing. The decision usually comes down to four costs that stack on top of each other.

Ongoing maintenance. A pool in Metro Atlanta typically costs several thousand dollars a year to run between chemicals, electricity, equipment repairs and resurfacing. Over ten years that spend approaches or exceeds the cost of removal, and it buys nothing back.

Structural repair. If the shell is cracked, the deck is settling, or the plumbing is failing, repair quotes can climb quickly. Once a repair quote approaches half the cost of a full removal, replacement stops making financial sense for most homeowners in Sandy Springs, Marietta or Kennesaw.

Safety and liability. A pool that is no longer used is still a drowning risk and an insurance liability. Filling it in removes the exposure entirely, which matters most for families with young children and for rental properties.

Usable land and resale. A removed pool returns the footprint to the yard. A partially removed pool must be disclosed at resale and cannot carry a structure, which buyers and lenders treat differently from a clean, buildable lot. That distinction shows up in Alpharetta and Roswell transactions more often than homeowners expect.

Atlanta Pool Removal FAQs

How much does pool removal cost in Atlanta?

Typical ranges for a medium-sized pool in Metro Atlanta are about $8,100 to $9,900 for a vinyl liner pool, $9,000 to $11,000 for fiberglass, and $10,800 to $13,200 for concrete or gunite. Above-ground pools are far cheaper, usually $2,250 to $2,750. Final pricing depends on shell thickness, backyard access, haul distance, and county permit requirements.

What is the difference between full pool demolition and partial pool removal?

A full pool demolition removes the entire concrete shell, rebar, and plumbing, then backfills in compacted layers so the footprint stays buildable. A partial removal breaks the top 24 to 36 inches of wall, punches drainage ports in the floor, drops the broken concrete into the basin, and fills the rest with soil. Partial removal costs less but must be disclosed at resale and prevents future building on that footprint.

Why is compaction testing critical for pool removal in North Georgia clay?

North Georgia sits on Cecil red clay, which swells when wet and shrinks and cracks when dry. Fill placed without mechanical compaction leaves air pockets that collapse over time, causing settlement and sinkholes. Backfill is tested for optimum moisture content and compacted in 6 to 8 inch lifts with vibratory equipment to reach at least 95 percent Modified Proctor density.

How is water pooling and a swampy yard prevented after pool removal?

If the concrete basin is left intact, it acts as an underground tub that traps water and turns the backfill into mud. The barrier is broken by punching 18 inch drainage ports through the pool floor every 4 feet, then placing a 12 to 18 inch base of washed #57 stone so groundwater drains into the water table instead of saturating the soil.

What permits are required in Fulton, Cobb, and DeKalb Counties?

Fulton, Cobb, and DeKalb Counties require a residential building or demolition permit for in-ground pool removal. Applications need a site plan showing property boundaries, setbacks, tree protection zones, and erosion control such as Type C silt fencing. Most counties require a rough inspection to confirm the floor is broken for drainage and a final inspection after grading.

Can I build a patio, deck, or guest house over a removed pool?

Yes, but only after a certified engineered full removal. That process extracts the concrete debris and backfills to structural density under engineer supervision, and produces a compaction report showing 95 percent Modified Proctor density, which building inspectors require before issuing permits for permanent structures. Partial removals are not suitable for structural loads.

How are trees and landscaping protected during pool demolition?

Heavy equipment compacts soil in the critical root zone and can kill mature hardwoods over time. Root zones should be mapped before machinery arrives, 1.5 inch polymer ground protection mats keep pressure under 4 PSI, and 36 inch mini-loaders allow access through narrow gates without removing fences.

How long does pool removal take in Atlanta?

Most residential pool removals take three to five working days on site once the permit is issued. Partial removals are faster, often finishing in about 72 hours on site. Permit review varies by county and can add one to three weeks before work begins. Weather and backyard access are the two biggest variables.
